# Big Ideas — week ending 2026-07-17

The week's center of gravity was the open-weight model race. Moonshot AI dropped **Kimi K3**, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open MoE, and Mira Murati's **Thinking Machines Lab** shipped its first open model, **Inkling** (975B), alongside the Tinker fine-tuning platform — two of the largest open releases to date, days apart. ICML 2026 in Seoul handed all three Outstanding Paper awards to diffusion-model work, led by "The Flexibility Trap," which argues arbitrary-order generation actually hurts reasoning in diffusion LMs. On the physical side, Lazard's 19th LCOE+ report delivered the week's most consequential number: standalone battery-storage costs **rose for the first time in years** as tariffs and FEOC rules cut access to cheap Chinese lithium-ion — a reversal of the cost-collapse narrative that has anchored this breakthrough.

Bio kept pace: Jennifer Doudna's lab used AI structure-based design to build **synthetic CRISPR nucleases with no natural counterpart** (Science), and two Nature papers showed the Cas12a2 enzyme can be programmed to shred the DNA of cancer cells while sparing healthy ones. **General Fusion** became the first pure-play fusion company on Nasdaq (GFUZ). Two theses cohered across independent voices — video-native "World Action Models" as the successor to VLAs, and a near-term regulatory cliff for open-weight models. Three new voices join the board: Anthropic interpretability lead **Jack Lindsey**, synthetic-cell pioneer **Kate Adamala**, and ICML awardee **Sarah Ball**.
