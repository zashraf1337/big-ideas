# Big Ideas — week ending 2026-10-09

_Run executed 2026-10-13 (scheduled run delayed); research window 2026-10-02 → 2026-10-09._

Open weights and price cuts led AI this week. Reflection AI announced Beam, a 501B-parameter MoE with 23B active, and Mistral released Large 4 (1.05T total, 49B active, 1M context). Anthropic shipped Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, and Google previewed a persistent enterprise Gemini agent that works across Workspace and the CLI. The biggest story was OpenAI's release of 372 AI-generated math result families, followed a day later by the withdrawal of three Hodge-conjecture papers over a sign error. Terence Tao responded by calling for a "Math 2.0", Daniel Litt (new voice) made the optimistic case, and an open letter urged mathematicians to stop working with OpenAI. In autonomy, Waymo closed its first debt raise ($5B), Tesla registered 150 more Cybercabs in Texas, and Boston Dynamics named ex-Amazon AI chief Rohit Prasad as CEO.

In bio, Novartis paid $575M upfront for Abogen's mRNA-encoded CD19xCD3 T-cell engager, a sign that mRNA platforms are being repriced for in vivo cell-engager work. The Nobel Prize in Medicine went to the inventors of optogenetics (Deisseroth, Hegemann, Nagel). Paradromics reported more than four years of stable recording in sheep, and Neuralink's pretrained decoders hit 11.32 bits/s. Kyverna is close to filing what would be the first BLA for a CAR-T in autoimmune disease, while Novartis and BMS have paused their trials. Petrelintide (amylin) hit its weight-loss endpoint but disappointed on A1C. In the physical domains it was a quieter week: a Jülich preprint found Quantinuum Helios-1 handles QEC-style mid-circuit measurement best, Pasqal was asked to lead a French programme on neutral-atom fault tolerance, and Bezos floated an eventual Blue Origin IPO.
