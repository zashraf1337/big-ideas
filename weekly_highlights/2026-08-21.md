# Big Ideas — week ending 2026-08-21

Bio took the week. Merck and Moderna reported that intismeran autogene plus Keytruda met both its primary recurrence-free-survival endpoint and its key secondary distant-metastasis-free-survival endpoint in Phase 3 INTerpath-001, in resected stage IIB–IV melanoma. That is the first positive Phase 3 any individualized mRNA neoantigen therapy has ever produced, and it converts the personalized-cancer-vaccine sub-theme from a promising Phase 2b story into a registrational one. Elsewhere in metabolic, the first genuinely head-to-head oral GLP-1 quarter landed and it was lopsided: oral semaglutide around $496M against orforglipron's $98M, with the efficacy gap doing the work. CRISPR Therapeutics quietly opened a Phase 1 on an editor architecture that isn't Cas9 at all.

On the AI side the interesting activity moved off the base models and onto post-training and harnesses. Z.ai shipped GLM-5.3 on the *same* GLM-5.2 base and moved Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6% to 28.3% on post-training alone — a clean data point for the argument that harness-and-post-training is where the near-term margin sits. DeepSeek and Prime Intellect both open-sourced fully pluggable agent harnesses in the same week (DeepSeek's tagline is literally "Everything is a Plugin"), which is the paradigm-layer signal this board is supposed to catch rather than the model-release layer. Award papers from ACL and ICML pinned down two concrete failure modes — indirect entropy control in RLVR, and diffusion LMs using arbitrary generation order to route around exactly the high-uncertainty tokens exploration needs. Capital events were real too: Etched at a $21B valuation with its first production rack actually delivered to Jane Street, Form Energy's $750M Series G against an 80 GWh iron-air backlog, and General Fusion becoming the first fusion developer that has to report quarterly.
