# Big Ideas — week ending 2026-09-18

Bio had the strongest week on the board. Beam Therapeutics reported that a single dose of BEAM-302, an in vivo adenine base editor correcting the SERPINA1 mutation, durably raised functional alpha-1 antitrypsin above the protective threshold across 29 dosed patients — the clearest durability signal yet for one-and-done in vivo editing in a common genetic disease. Alongside it, the FDA cleared a Phase 1b/2 of Abelzeta's dual CD20/BCMA CAR-T in progressive MS, NEJM published 16-patient data on a lentiviral vector that manufactures CD19 CAR-T cells directly inside the body (no ex vivo step, no lymphodepletion), and a UCSF group reported the first BCI to decode speech and upper-body gesture simultaneously rather than switching between modes. Three of those four are about delivery rather than editing chemistry, which is the theme worth watching.

On the AI side the discourse outran the releases. Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" landed Sep 12 and drew same-day responses from Altman, Musk and Hassabis, then critical readings from Ben Thompson and Zvi Mowshowitz; Kapoor and Narayanan published the counterweight two days later, arguing the recent loss-of-control incidents read better as operational and security failures than as existential signal. Shipping-wise: Sakana's Fugu Max/Ultra v2 is notable less for the 74.3 DeepSWE score than for the shape — an orchestrator sold as a model, routing one request across a pool. Shanghai AI Lab put a 744B agentic MoE under MIT license. The Linux Foundation and Agentic AI Foundation launched the first vendor-neutral MCP certification, which is what protocol maturity looks like from the outside. In physical sciences, QUOPS finally put a number on the quantum gap — Quantinuum Helios-1 at Q=1,504 physical against the Q≈3×10^8 needed for RSA-2048 — and CATL began selling 587Ah LFP storage cells direct to buyers at roughly $65/kWh.
