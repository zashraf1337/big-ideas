# Big Ideas — week ending 2026-06-19

An exceptionally dense week, and the single biggest move was financial rather than scientific: **SpaceX went public**, pricing its IPO at $135/share on Jun 11 and opening on Nasdaq as **SPCX** on Jun 12, closing ~19% up at ~$161 for a roughly **$2.1T valuation — the largest IPO in history**. Biology delivered two genuine genetic-medicine firsts in the same week: **Intellia's lonvo-z** became the **first in vivo CRISPR therapy to win a positive pivotal Phase 3** (hereditary angioedema, 87% attack reduction, NEJM), and **Prime Medicine's PM577a** secured the **first-ever clinical authorization for an in vivo prime editor** (Wilson Disease). Add a unanimous 9-0 FDA advisory-committee vote for Moderna's mRNA seasonal flu vaccine, an at-home ALS speech BCI in Nature Medicine, and CD19 CAR-T showing early remissions in refractory lupus, and the bio slate alone would have made the week.

In AI, the story was open weights and embodiment. **Z.ai's GLM-5.2** became the first open-weight model past 80% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, **Google shipped DiffusionGemma** (an open text-diffusion LLM), and the week's loudest cross-voice thesis was **"world models, not LLM scaling, are the road to embodied intelligence"** — pushed by Jim Fan, Yann LeCun (AMI Labs), and Fei-Fei Li, with NVIDIA's **DreamZero** as the falsifiable evidence and **Sherry Yang** (NYU/DeepMind) added as a new tracked voice anchoring it. On the physical side, **QuEra + AWS** committed to a 256-logical-qubit fault-tolerant machine ("Libra") on Braket by 2028, **QuantumScape** signed **Honda** as its second solid-state OEM, and **D-Wave** pivoted to a dual-rail gate-model fault-tolerance roadmap. Net coverage moved a lot: **14 gap sub-themes flipped gap→active** (77 active / 51 gap, +14), with zero new proposed categories.
