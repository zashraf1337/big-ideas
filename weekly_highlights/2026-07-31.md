# Big Ideas — week ending 2026-07-31

The AI-infrastructure and AI-safety layers carried the week. The Model Context Protocol shipped its largest revision to date (stateless request/response core, Multi Round-Trip Requests, header-based routing, RFC 9207 auth hardening, all Tier-1 SDKs same day), and Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 posted new coding/agentic SOTA at roughly half Fable 5's cost. Against that optimism, a Princeton/UK-AISI/Stanford study handed frontier agents six days and real compute to advance two unpublished papers — the agents finished the engineering but were unambiguously rejected by the original human authors for poor judgment and weak backtracking, and a US policy push to restrict open-weight frontier models gained visible momentum (Lambert's Interconnects essay, Lawfare's "knives are out," and a joint Nvidia/Microsoft/Meta warning).

Physical sciences delivered the concrete milestones. IBM and the University of Chicago claimed verifiable quantum advantage on error-corrected logical circuits; SpaceX's Starship Flight 13 deployed the first Starlink V3 satellites and returned an intact upper stage after reentry; and LONGi set a 35.5% ESTI-certified silicon-perovskite tandem-cell record. Two long-standing gap sub-themes flipped active this week — iron-air multi-day storage (Ore Energy's ~1 GWh offtake with Dutch utility Budget Thuis) and co-packaged optics / silicon photonics (a foundry mass-production inflection) — dropping total gaps from 8 to 6 and taking ENERGY to 100% coverage. Bio added an experiment-guided AlphaFold3 for measurement-consistent structural ensembles, a CRISPR/dCas13 RNA-editing immuno-oncology result, and Moderna's mRNA-1010 flu vaccine heading to an Aug 5 FDA decision.
