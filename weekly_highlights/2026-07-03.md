# Big Ideas — week ending 2026-07-03

A packed, well-sourced week dominated by frontier-model launches and unusually strong gap-filling. OpenAI opened a **government-vetted limited preview of the GPT-5.6 family** — Sol (flagship, new SOTA on Terminal-Bench 2.1), Terra and Luna — with broad release gated by a US-government access process tied to a forthcoming cyber Executive Order, while **Anthropic shipped Claude Sonnet 5** at introductory $2/$10 per-M pricing. The single most striking research result: an LLM+Lean-4 feedback loop using **Claude Fable 5 produced the first machine-verified proof of the decade-old Farhi-Goldstone-Gutmann QAOA conjecture** (MIT; arXiv 2606.29687), certified end-to-end in Lean 4 — a concrete data point for AI-assisted mathematics.

Bio and the physical sciences filled long-dormant gaps. Moderna's **5-year KEYNOTE-942** data showed its personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine sustains a 49% recurrence/death reduction; **China approved the first commercial invasive BCI** (Neuracle's NEO); and **Grail's Galleri MCED trial missed its primary endpoint** in 143,000 UK adults — a sobering signal for population screening. On the hardware/energy/space side: **NASA's ~$590M CLPS Moon Base awards**, an **HZB 25.5% perovskite-CIGS tandem record**, **NVIDIA's fully liquid-cooled AI servers**, **IQM's 1,000x "directional tile" QEC codes**, and **SpaceX signaling a consumer Starlink mobile play**. Three theses recurred across independent voices: local open-weight coding agents as a real Claude Code/Codex substitute, AI-monetization data undercutting the "bubble" narrative, and the frontier-access/monetization-window policy debate. Ten gap sub-themes flipped to active — QUANTUM is now fully covered.
